How the numbers are made

Both dashboards run the same calculations as the Python engines below, in the browser. The Python and browser results match to the dollar on the sample data.

Sheet 01 · NEM billing

  1. Baseline. Every interval's import is priced at its TOU energy rate. Demand charges take the monthly maximum import (all hours), the 4–9pm peak, and in summer the part-peak.
  2. NEM 2.0. Exports earn the retail TOU rate minus non-bypassable charges. Credits roll forward month to month and offset energy charges only; demand and customer charges are paid monthly. Unused credit is forfeited at true-up, and any net-surplus kWh earns net surplus compensation.
  3. NEM 3.0 (NBT). Same netting and true-up, but each exported kWh earns the ACC value for its month and hour. Midday exports earn a few cents; summer evenings earn the most.
  4. Solar + BESS. Net load after the battery is billed under NBT. With an Energy Toolbase export, the file's battery column is used as-is. Otherwise the built-in dispatch charges only from surplus solar and flattens the 4–9pm peak by day-ahead water-filling.

With 15-minute data, billed demand uses the 15-minute peak. Hourly data understates demand charges, most for peaky loads.

Sheet 02 · Asset performance

  1. Expected output. PVWatts-style: Sandia open-rack module temperature, −0.35%/°C, 10% DC losses, 98% inverter efficiency, clipped at the AC rating.
  2. Losses. Availability = expected × (1 − inverter availability). Soiling = the daily soiling ratio applied to available energy. Curtailment = the unconstrained estimate minus delivered, during setpoint hours. Other is the remainder.
  3. ASTM E2848. Reporting conditions from the filtered window (60th-percentile POA, mean ambient and wind), OLS on measured and on modeled power, capacity ratio = measured ÷ modeled at reporting conditions.
  4. Battery. Round-trip efficiency at the PCS and including auxiliary energy, availability by day, equivalent full cycles, state of health from quarterly capacity tests.
